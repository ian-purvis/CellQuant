# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **QuantAnalysis XY tables, linear regression and correlation**: **File → New XY table** holds an X column and Y data sets with replicates side by side (type, paste, rename, add rows, data sets and replicates). **Linear regression…** fits a line to each data set (every replicate as a point, or row means; optionally through the origin): slope, intercept and X-intercept with SE and CI, R², Sy.x and whether the slope differs from zero, with the data sets side by side, and with two or more data sets whether their slopes (then intercepts) are equal. **Correlation (XY)…** gives Pearson or Spearman r with its CI and P. The **XY** graph shows each data set as means with error bars or every replicate, the fitted lines and their confidence or prediction bands. XY tables and their analyses are saved in projects.
- **QuantAnalysis grouped graphs**: graphs of a grouped table colour each data set the same in every row and lay the cells out **Interleaved** (one block per row, data sets side by side, legend above) or **Separated** (one block per data set, its name under the block), with a gap between blocks; empty cells keep their place and formats are set per data set. A two-way ANOVA linked to the graph draws its multiple comparisons as brackets within rows or data sets, or between row or data set means. A grouped table's suggested setup adds an interleaved bar graph. **Join means** joins each data set's means across rows. Legends placed outside the plot (right, or the row above) now get room on the page instead of being cut off.
- **QuantAnalysis grouped tables and two-way ANOVA**: **File → New grouped table** (rows × data sets, replicates side by side; type, paste, rename, add rows, data sets and replicates) or import results with a second factor (**Data sets by**). **Two-way ANOVA…** reports the row, column and interaction effects (Type III sums of squares, so unequal cell sizes are handled; additive model when the interaction cannot be fitted), with multiple comparisons within each row or data set (one family per row or all in one family) or of the main effects (least-squares means). Grouped tables and their analyses are saved in projects.
- **QuantAnalysis page layouts**: **Graph → New page layout** puts several graphs on one page for a figure: US Letter, A4 or a custom size, a grid of cells with margins and gaps, panel letters (A, B, C… or a, b, c…) and an optional title. Graphs keep their own size and font sizes and are shrunk only when larger than their cell (a note says so). The page follows its graphs and their data, is saved in the project, and exports as SVG (graphs stay vector), PNG, TIFF or JPEG at 300 or 600 dpi.
- **QuantAnalysis data processing**: **Analyze → Data processing** and **Process ▾** above a data table make a new table that follows its source: **Transform** (Y × K, ÷, +, −, powers, square root, reciprocal, log10/log2/ln, 10^Y, e^Y, |Y|, logit, arcsine, z-score), **Normalize** (0% and 100% from the smallest/first value or a number and the largest/last value, sum or a number), **Fraction of total** (column, grand or row total), **Remove baseline** (difference, ratio, % of or % change from a baseline column, by its mean or row by row) and **Column math** (A − B, A ÷ B, A + B, A × B, % change, log2 ratio). Values with no result are left blank and counted in the notes. **Change processing…** changes the options; projects save the link. The list of recent projects is now kept as JSON text, so a path with a comma or a damaged setting no longer breaks it.
- **Step 3, circle tools**: **Circle** (with **Add new objects**) places a round object where you click, or drag from the centre to set its size; it fills only empty pixels, gets a new number and is recorded at once. **Delete circle** (with **Delete objects**) deletes every object lying entirely inside the circle; objects crossing its edge stay. **Size** sets the diameter and starts at the median object size of the image. One **Undo** reverses either. Circles placed while the last one is still being recorded are all recorded.
- **Step 3, clicks pick objects more reliably**: a click with a little hand shake still deletes or selects the object under it instead of being taken as a drag, and napari's pan tool is put back on the layer in use, so a paint or pick tool left on no longer takes the click.
- **Step 5, Edit cells**: **Edit cells…** opens a window for calling single cells positive or negative by hand, overriding the cutoff for those cells only. Select cells by clicking them (Shift+click adds or removes), drawing around them (**Select in area**) or by call (**Select all…**: positive, negative, called by hand), then **Positive**, **Negative** or **Use cutoff** for the marker shown; a table sets any marker's call for the selected cells. Calls survive cutoff changes, are saved with the analysis per image (`cell_calls`, kept out of `recipe_sha256`), are dropped when their cell moved or was deleted, and are included in the approval: a call clears an approval only if the percent positive changes. Cells called by hand get a yellow diamond in the image; `objects.csv` gains `<marker>_pos_by_hand` for markers with calls and `image_summary.csv` gains `n_called_by_hand`, and the provenance file `hand_calls_sha256`. Calls are made one marker at a time (not while all markers are shown together). Step 5 counts and the Images list count the calls.
- **Step 5, All markers**: with two or more markers, **Display objects by → All markers** shows every marker's histogram and cutoff slider for the image at once, colors each object by the exact set of markers it is positive for (each marker its own color, each overlap such as double or triple positive a color of its own, all editable and remembered on this computer), and lists every mix with its count and percent, with a **Show** tick per mix. **Apply to all images** there copies this image's cutoffs for every marker; **Pop out** moves the controls to their own window. Step 6 also lists the objects positive for each set of two or more markers that no report already counts (for example triple positives). The saved results are unchanged.
- **QuantAnalysis projects**: **File → Save project**, **Save project as…**, **Open project…** and **Open recent** keep every data table, analysis and graph in one `.cqstats` file (a zip archive holding `project.json`). Tables keep their values and import choices; analyses are saved as their options and computed again on opening; graphs keep their whole format. A table imported from the open experiment is imported again when the project is opened with that experiment open, and otherwise follows its results folder. The title bar shows the file and `*` for unsaved changes; **New project**, **Open project…** and closing CellQuant ask to save them. A graph style that cannot be saved (for example a read-only settings folder) now says so instead of failing.
- **QuantAnalysis, formatting graphs**: the format panel is now collapsible sections (Graph, Data sets, Axes, Title and legend, Text boxes and arrows, Significance, Styles), and **Format graph…** / **Format axes…** open the same settings as tabs; every change redraws at once. Per data set: symbol shape, size, fill and border colour, border width, opacity and hollow symbols; bar fill, opacity, hatch pattern, border and width; box and violin fill and outline; mean or median line colour, width and length; error bars in both directions or above only, with colour, thickness and cap width; before-after and mean-joining lines; show or hide each part, and **Apply to all data sets**. Axes: plain, offset (the default), boxed or no frame; per axis range, reverse, linear/log 10/log 2/ln scale, tick interval, minor ticks, tick direction and length, number format (decimals, thousands separator, scientific or power notation, %, prefix and suffix), axis titles with font, size, bold, italic, colour and markup for Greek letters, superscripts and subscripts; Y axis breaks in 2 or 3 segments; a right Y axis; column labels turned, wrapped or hidden. A graph title, a legend (inside, outside, or one row above as a master legend; frame and order), free text boxes, arrows, lines and brackets, and the significance brackets' line width, gap, end length and star size. **Click any part of the graph** to select it and open its section; double-click opens its dialog; drag text boxes, arrows and the legend to move them. **Styles**: built-in Editorial, Classic (offset axes, bold, black and white) and Colour-blind safe (Okabe-Ito), styles saved per user in `graph_styles.json` beside CellQuant's per-computer settings, **Make this graph look like…** (choosing what to copy and onto which graphs), and **Copy format** / **Paste format**. All settings live in a `GraphFormat` record that converts to and from JSON, ready for project files; graphs made before open with their settings. Exports still match the screen at the chosen size. The "2nd" reference-colour button is no longer squeezed.
- **QuantAnalysis, three or more groups**: **Analyze → Compare three or more columns…** (ordinary, Welch and Brown-Forsythe, repeated-measures ANOVA, Kruskal-Wallis and Friedman; multiple comparisons for every pair, against one or two reference groups such as a negative and a positive control, selected pairs or a linear trend, with the methods valid for the design), **Identify outliers…** (ROUT, Grubbs', iterative Grubbs'; shaded in the table, hollow on graphs, and a cleaned copy of the table that follows the original) and **Analyze a stack of P values…**. **Help me choose…** now recommends tests for three or more groups and fills the new dialog, and the suggested setup runs Kruskal-Wallis with Dunn's tests against the first group. Graphs show significance brackets, asterisks above columns (colour per reference group) or compact letters. The Windows launcher checks for the statistics libraries with `python -m cellquant.stats._deps_check` instead of a `python -c` string that conda or PowerShell could mangle.
- **QuantAnalysis**, a statistics window modelled on the standard column-table workflow, in its own window with napari's dark theme. Open it from step 6 (**Analyze…**, next to Export: the open experiment, followed live, so a changed cutoff or approval re-imports its tables) or with **Analyze a results folder…** (Start page and step 1) for a saved results or export folder. **Import CellQuant results** picks the measure (a report's percent, `total_objects`, `count_area`, or a per-object value), what a dot is (biological replicate N by default when there is a replicate folder, pooled as in `units_by_<unit>.csv`; image or object, technical replicate n, with a pseudoreplication warning for objects), the groups and their order, and approved images only. Column statistics, normality and lognormality tests, and **Compare two columns…** (unpaired, Welch, lognormal, paired, ratio paired, Mann-Whitney, Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Wilcoxon) give results sheets with warnings (including a small-N warning for rank tests) and a methods sentence, recomputed when the table is edited. **Help me choose…** recommends a test; **Use suggested setup** builds the suggested analysis for image data. Graphs: column scatter, bar, box, violin, before-after, estimation plot and SuperPlot, with a P bracket; exported as SVG, PDF, EPS, PNG, JPEG or LZW TIFF at 300/600/1200 dpi, sized in mm or inches. Needs `pip install "cellquant[stats]"`, which the installers now include. Guide: **Help → Statistics guide…** (docs/STATISTICS.md).
- **3D tile overlap** (step 2, Advanced, true 3D only): how much Cellpose's 256 px tiles overlap, 0.05 to 0.5. 0.1 stays the default (Cellpose's own); lower runs fewer tiles on large images, with a little more risk of seams. Saved as `tile_overlap_3d` only when changed, so earlier settings and their saved objects are unchanged, and recorded in provenance with the other full 3D settings (`segmentation_engine.full_3d.tile_overlap`).
- **Faster switching between images**: Previous / Next (steps 2 to 6) keep napari's layers and give them the next image's pixels and objects, instead of removing every layer and adding it again, which was almost all of the time a switch took. An image with other channels or another size still gets new layers. What is shown is unchanged.
- **Large experiments**: the masks of all but the most recently used results (about 512 MB) are moved to a temporary file and read back when needed, so memory no longer grows with every image opened or run. Saving writes only results that changed (before, every save rewrote every open result), and export no longer holds every image's masks at once.
- **Crop works on Z-stacks** (Cellpose; Link slices and full 3D): the region is found on the max projection and every slice is kept inside the rectangles, so 3D runs faster when only part of the image holds the cells. Classical on a stack still uses the whole image. Each image with the crop box ticked adds one line to the **Log** (e.g. `Crop: 38% of the frame (3 regions), 0.4 s to find`). Stacks analyzed before with the box ticked were analyzed in full; analyzing them again crops them, and an approval on such an image is cleared with a message saying why.
- **Step 5, cutoff per image**: the Cutoff slider, histogram, **Auto** and **Reset cutoff** change the image shown only (the *Apply to* menu is gone). **Apply to all images** (bottom of step 5) gives every image this image's cutoff; when images are already approved it asks whether to change them too or only the unapproved ones. Images run before the markers were set up get their markers measured when shown, so the positive/negative overlay appears after **Approve** opens the next image.
- **Step 3, click to delete**: **Delete objects** turns the cursor into a delete tool; each click on an object deletes it (**Undo** brings it back; **Back** or Esc stops). **Restore object** and **Delete object** are gone; **Undo all** (formerly **Reset image**) reverses every edit on the image. Deleting no longer switches the selected layer to *Object IDs*.
- **Step 3, Keep only**: outline an area with **Draw area**, then **Keep only** deletes every object that does not touch it (one **Undo** brings them all back), for clearing large regions where nothing real was found.
- **No resizing by default** (step 2, Cellpose-SAM): images are segmented at their own size instead of being scaled so nuclei are about 30 px. Much faster for small nuclei (a 6 µm nucleus at 0.57 µm/px was enlarged about 2.9 times per side). **Resize to nucleus size** (Advanced) brings the old behaviour back; 2D settings saved before keep resizing (`resize_to_diameter`, written when off, and always in true 3D). True 3D always starts unticked, including settings saved before; ticking it there is remembered. Nucleus diameter still sizes the full 3D voxel cutoff; classic Cellpose always resizes. `diameter_px_used` is empty when nothing was resized.
- **Load results…** (step 1): open objects found elsewhere (a cluster, Cellpose, another CellQuant experiment) over their original images. Pick the results folder: the original images are added from the folder's `images.csv` (`name`, `source_file`, `position`) or the images CellQuant saved results for, found by file name in a folder you choose if they moved, or by the masks' names. Masks are TIFFs of object numbers matched to images by name (`<image>_masks.tif`, `_cp_masks`, `_labels`; subfolders searched). A cluster package's channel and Z handling (`prep.json`) become step 2's settings; otherwise Z is set to fit the masks. Loading only attaches the masks (about a second for 30 images); markers are measured when an image is opened or results are exported, and later edits and runs keep the loaded objects. Masks of the wrong size or Z are listed, not used.
- **Step 2, Channel per image**: images listed by folder; select any images (or a folder) and give them their own channel in **Set channel…**, for example mCherry for some and GFP for others. Images left on *Default* use the **Default channel** (formerly Source channel). One run does them all; the export's `segmentation_channel` column names each image's own channel. Replaces the By image group rows.
- **Count area** (step 5, optional): draw polygons on the image after objects are found and use them for this image or all images; only objects whose center is inside are counted. Objects outside stay in `objects.csv` marked `in_count_area = False`; `image_summary.csv` gains `count_area` (µm², or px² without a pixel size). Saved with the experiment. Separate from cropping in step 2, which limits where objects are found.
- **Cellpose-SAM precision and CPU threads from the hardware**: bfloat16 only where the GPU or CPU computes it natively, otherwise float32 (recorded in provenance; recipe key `precision` overrides). The installer times Cellpose at a few CPU thread counts and later runs use the fastest. Restored from unpublished local work.
- **Crop to the region of interest** (step 2, off by default, 2D analyses): finds the area holding the positive cells on the ticked channels (by default the reports' denominator, e.g. the reporter), grows it by a margin (50 µm), and finds objects only inside rectangles around it. Brightness scaling and cutoffs come from the whole image; an image whose region covers more than 70% of it is analyzed in full. Objects touching a crop edge are flagged (`at_crop_edge`), review outlines the rectangles, and the Plan's right-click menu has **Use the full image** per image. Restored from unpublished local work.
- **Step 4**: the marker cutoff is a slider with an editable number. Positive objects are green and negative ones magenta by default (a pair that stays distinct with red-green color blindness); **Positive color** / **Negative color** change them.
- **Clicking an object selects it** for **Delete object** / **Restore object**. Nothing is deleted until an object has been clicked (before, Delete object could remove object 1).
- **Problems appear in a red box** above the steps: failed runs (with each failed image and its reason), images that cannot be shown or opened, and settings that cannot be loaded. A run that stops on an unexpected error no longer leaves the window locked; the details go to `cellquant_developer.log`.
- **Plain words throughout**: menus such as "Cellpose (AI model)" and "Automatic (Otsu)"; *cutoff* rather than threshold; *settings* rather than recipe; the Measurements and Markers tables show channel names and plain statistics; result rows are chosen from menus (**Count** … **among** …). Runs are summarized the same way everywhere ("7 finished, 1 needs a look, 1 failed"), and step 1's Status column tells failed images from ones that need a look.
- **Channel layers** are labeled from each image's own file (*Channel 1 = Far Red*), and step 1 shows that image's channel order; contrast is remembered per channel, matched by name.
- **Step 1**: Browse picks the highlighted folder on Windows and confirms it; edits save automatically (no Save button); Add images/Add folder, Include shown and metadata columns are under **Advanced**.
- **Step 2**: **Typical nucleus diameter (µm)** (default 6) sets Cellpose's size and a 5 µm² debris floor; Z-stack mode stays usable after a run.
- **Nucleus diameter (µm) per image**: each image converts it with its own µm/pixel when it runs (before, the first image's pixel size was used for all), so 20x and 40x images get the same physical size; the pixel diameter used is in provenance (`diameter_px_used`). A diameter in pixels (Advanced) is still the same in every image.
- **Set sizes (µm)** can set the pixel size for this image and every image without one (the default), this image only, or every included image.
- **Use GPU** is ticked automatically when a usable NVIDIA GPU is found and the settings do not say otherwise. The GPU banner names a GPU that PyTorch cannot use and says how to fix it.
- **Navigation**: Previous / Next stop at the first and last image, the bottom bar says when they go through only failed images or images that need a look, and an experiment with no included images says so.
- Step 5's advanced section no longer repeats the run and export buttons; **Run all images** says "(this analysis)" when there are several analyses.
- ND2 files on OneDrive that are not downloaded yet are read through an ordinary open file.
- `cellquant run` has help text and a plain summary.
- New `docs/DEVELOPERS.md` and `tests/README.md`; `docs/STATUS.md` starts with the known limitations.
- **Per-unit summaries** (step 6, Advanced: **Group by**, **Unit**): export adds `units_by_<unit>.csv` (each unit's images pooled, for example per retina; by default the folder that holds the images) and, when grouped, `grouped_by_<group>_equal_units.csv` (the mean of the units' percents, so every retina counts once, with n units and SD). Restored from unpublished local work.

## 2.1.0 (2026-09-30)

- **Several analyses of the same images.** An experiment can hold several analyses, each with its own settings,
  results, deleted objects and approvals: for example one per channel that objects are found in, or two
  segmentation methods. The **Analysis** list at the top of the panel chooses, adds (**New analysis…**,
  **One per channel…**), renames and removes them. **Run all analyses** runs every included image with each
  analysis in turn (with Pause and Cancel); **Export all analyses…** saves each in its own folder plus
  `all_analyses_image_summary.csv`. Experiments made with 2.0.0 open with their settings as the first analysis.
- **The Plan** (button **Plan…**): a dock listing every image grouped by channel layout, by folder, or both,
  in a grid (images × analyses) or a tree (image ▸ analyses). Tick which images each analysis runs, one cell,
  a group, an analysis or everything at once; choose the channel to find objects in for single images; see
  each image's status per analysis; double-click to open an image with an analysis. **Run all analyses** runs
  the plan.
- **Other channel layouts**: an image whose file stores the channels in another order is segmented and
  measured in the channels of the same names; images without such a channel are marked for checking.
- **Duplicate** (advanced, step 5) now makes a new analysis and keeps the old one and its results.

## 2.0.0 (2026-09-29)

CellQuant 2 is a rewrite of the original CellQuant. There was no 1.x release: version 1 ended at 0.4.0a3,
which stays available on the `v1` branch and at tag `v0.4.0a3`. Settings files and results from version 1 do
not open in version 2.

- One guided napari window: images, finding nuclei, markers, checking, results, with a success check per step.
- Segmentation: classical thresholding, Cellpose 3 and Cellpose-SAM; 2D (max projection or one slice) and 3D
  (linked slices or whole volume) analysis of z-stacks; ND2 and TIFF input, including folders of retinas.
- Markers: positive by mean brightness, or by the percent of a nucleus whose pixels are at or above a pixel
  level (the `positive_fraction` rule of version 1); reports such as (Fluor+ OTX2+) / Fluor+.
- Review: delete, restore and draw objects, undo, approve; results are re-measured without segmenting again.
- Experiments: relocatable folders, saved runs, settings fingerprints, exports for spreadsheets or R.
- Sweeps: compare segmentation methods, Z modes and thresholds across images, scored against hand counts.
- Windows installer with one environment per Cellpose engine.
- MIT license, citation file.
